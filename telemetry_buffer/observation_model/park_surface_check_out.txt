Cabin/public gradient: 11.2x
Same gradient without routine cleaning: 14.5x
Park observed gradient: roughly 100-300x
Cleaning moves the gradient the wrong way: it removes more from a quiet cabin
than from a busy public zone, where hand pickup already clears the pool faster
than housekeeping can. The gradient shortfall is therefore not a cleaning gap.

Shedder-hour asymmetry required for a 100x gradient, holding the implemented constants fixed:
  needed shedder-hours(cabin)/shedder-hours(public) = 29.33
  A cabin cannot accumulate that many more shedder-hours than a public zone;
  the gradient is therefore not reachable by hand transfer at any occupancy.

f is the fraction of a host's vomiting episodes that occur in its own cabin.
No study measures it. It is NOT a model parameter and the model does not use
it: emesis is deposited wherever the host is, which is f implied by the
schedule rather than declared. This sweep exists to report how much of the
gradient rests on an unmeasured behavioural quantity. Reading f off Park's
gradient would be fitting, and is refused.
