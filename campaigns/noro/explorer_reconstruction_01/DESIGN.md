# NORO Explorer reconstruction 01 — design (Paper-1 WP3)

**Date:** 2026-11-20
**Pathogens:** norwalk_gi
**Status:** design frozen before any cell runs; campaign gated on the declared Paper-1 freeze **and on the pre-freeze blocker below**
**Context:** `docs/norovirus/paper1_simulation_plan_assessment_2026_11_20.md` (item 2), `docs/norovirus/paper1_freeze_design_contract_2026_11_20.md` (§2 block, limitation 11), `docs/norovirus/wp0_explorer_platform_session_prompt_2026_11_20.md`

## Question

Does the frozen engine, given the documented introduction, reproduce the timing by role of the *Explorer of the Seas* outbreak of February 26 – March 5, 2006: a passenger onset peak on February 28 and a crew onset peak two days later?

The campaign scores the engine against the empirical curves. It fits nothing: no constant, mode or seed is chosen by how the comparison comes out, and a miss is a measurement.

## Pre-freeze blocker: Explorer does not resolve the same mechanisms as the archetypes

The frozen block requests `transmission.exposure_cap.enabled: true`. The engine applies the cap only where `platform_has_rhythm_catalog(platform_id)` holds, that is, where the platform id is listed in `data/rhythm/event_catalogs.json`. The four archetype hulls are listed. `explorer_seas_06` is not. The same test gates the rhythm layer (`SHIP-RHYTHM-02`, `rhythm.enabled: true` in `crusher_labs/config.yaml`, not a key of the frozen block), which deals each day's event schedule.

| Mechanism | Requested | `mega_cruise_5000` | `explorer_seas_06` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exposure cap (`EXPO-CAP-01`) | on (frozen block) | active | **inactive** |
| Rhythm layer (`SHIP-RHYTHM-02`) | on (config default, unpinned) | active | **inert** |

Measured by the cell worker's `effective_state` read-back (`tx_core._exposure_cap_active`, `engine._rhythm`) at initialization, seed 8105; recorded in `LEDGER.md`.

So the same requested block is a different effective engine on Explorer. A platform-class label does not change this: the gate reads the platform id. Until it is resolved, an Explorer cell is not a cell of the frozen reference, and an Explorer-against-mega difference would mix this in with geometry and complement.

The campaign encodes the blocker instead of hiding it. Every archive records the requested and effective state, and the readout treats "exposure cap requested but inactive" as an audit defect. As authored, every Explorer cell fails that audit, so no clean reconstruction table can be produced.

Two ways out, both outside this campaign and neither taken here:

1. **A reviewed eligibility correction.** Make Explorer eligible for the cap without importing an activity schedule: adding `explorer_seas_06` to the mega catalog's `platforms` list would also switch on the rhythm layer with mega's day program and a venue map naming mega zones, which is the import the review rules out. A cap-only eligibility is an engine or catalog change, so it is a moratorium-exception decision with per-hull effective-state assertions (the read-back above is that assertion).
2. **Explorer as a deferred exception.** The freeze proceeds on the four archetypes; the reconstruction waits until eligibility is decided.

The rhythm-layer difference is not covered by either option as worded and needs its own answer: there is no Explorer day program in the tree.

## Timing convention

February 26 – March 5 is eight calendar dates and seven elapsed days. The cells run 192 epochs, one 24-epoch model day per calendar date, with epoch 0 at the start of February 26. This is a declared inclusive-day approximation: the model ship carries its full complement for all of the embarkation date and all of the disembarkation date, which adds up to one day of onboard exposure that the voyage did not have.

Bounds on what that can move, all reported by the readout:

- The lag is a difference of two peak days and does not depend on how model days are aligned to dates.
- Peak days are reported exactly and within one day of the target, which covers a one-day alignment shift in either direction.
- Onset totals on Explorer are reported over all eight model days and over the first seven; the difference is the most the extra day can add. The target has no onsets on March 5 in either role.

## Declared introduction

The record: five passenger surveys recorded illness before boarding; CDC judged that people ill before boarding propagated disease person to person; no common dining location among ill crew. The surveys sampled cases and controls, so five is what was recorded, not a manifest count.

Declared as `initiation.explicit_seeds`, additive with the boarding draw:

```json
{"pathogen": "norwalk_gi", "count": 5, "role": "passenger", "epoch": 0, "onset_day": -1.0, "infection_age_days": 2.2}
```

- `count`, `role`, `epoch` are the record.
- `onset_day: -1.0` and `infection_age_days: 2.2` are **declared assumptions**. The record gives no onset date for the five. Onset is placed one day before embarkation, inside the VSP 4.1.1.2 three-day lookback; acquisition is one profile-median incubation (1.2 days) before that. Neither is swept, and neither may be revised after a cell is read.
- The seeds are composed onto the run spec by `cell.py` from the manifest's `introduction_blocks`, so the tier's `config_overrides` stays the frozen block.

**Limitation: the embarkation report counter cannot carry the five.** `preboarding_reportable_ids` is filled only by the VSP 4.1.1.2 reportable clause, which is crew-only: `engines/initiation.py` rejects `preboarding_assessment.passenger.reportable = true` at load. Bucket zero of the reporting curve therefore holds crew embarkation declarations only. The five passengers enter the onset line list on day 0 and report, if they report, through the sick-call ladder. The readout labels bucket zero as embarkation declarations and reports onsets both with and without the declared seeds. Letting a passenger declaration count at embarkation would be an engine change; it is recorded here as a possible moratorium-exception decision and is not made.

## Curve extraction

- **Onsets:** `run.sentinel_line_list`, per-person `onset_epoch` and `crew`. `onset_epoch` is the first epoch the person is observed symptomatic, clamped to ≥ 1, so a host symptomatic at embarkation lands on day 0. Binned to voyage days with the run's own clock; day 0 is the embarkation day (Feb 26). Two curves per role: all onsets, and onsets excluding the declared seeds.
- **Reporting:** per-epoch `cumulative_reported_cases_{passenger,crew}`, read at the last epoch of each day, with the epoch-0 value kept separately. The line list's `report_epoch` is a detection-channel epoch and is not used as the reporting curve.

## Comparison metrics

Computed by `readout.py`. Peaks and the lag use onsets excluding the declared seeds.

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Peak day per role | Day of the maximum daily onset count; the earliest day on a tie; ties counted | Passengers 2, crew 4 |
| Lag | Crew peak day − passenger peak day | 2 |
| Onset total per role (Explorer tiers) | Line-list onsets over the eight model days, and over the first seven | 252, 20 |
| Onset attack rate per role | Onset total ÷ the cell's own complement | 252/3,245, 20/1,183 |
| Reported-case attack rate per role | Final cumulative reported ÷ complement | Posting 243/3,245, 19/1,184 (cross-posting only) |
| Curve distance (reported, never judged) | Total variation between normalized daily curves | — |

**Evaluable cells.** A peak day is defined only where a curve exists: a cell is evaluable when it has at least 20 passenger onsets and at least 5 crew onsets, excluding the declared seeds. The thresholds are set here, before any cell runs, and are not derived from model output. The evaluable fraction is itself reported.

**Reading.** For each of lag, passenger peak day, crew peak day and the two onset totals, the readout reports the seed distribution on evaluable cells (median, 5th and 95th percentiles), the share of cells equal to the target, and whether the target lies in the central 90%. "Reproduces" means the target lies in the central 90% on `exp_declared_five`. `P(lag ≥ 1)` is reported beside it. No other statement is a criterion.

**Explorer against mega.** Differences are reported on matched seed labels. The two hulls differ in geometry and in complement (and here in passenger–crew split), so a difference is attributed to the bundle. This campaign cannot attribute it to geometry.

## Audit invariants

A violation is a defect in the cell, not a failed criterion: the frozen-block witness reports no mismatch; the cell ran 192 epochs; the declared arms seeded exactly five passengers and the baseline arms none; the initialized role counts equal the intended ones; the exposure cap is active wherever the block requests it; no passenger is in the embarkation report counter; with `--expect-sha`, the cell's engine SHA is the named reference.

## Response procedures and closure mapping

**Decision: the reconstruction does not rely on SOP-007 or SOP-009, no closure alias is added, and `protocols.json` is not edited.** The mismatch still matters whenever a procedure activates, including in the nominal baseline, so it is recorded here in full.

Zone ids named by the standing protocols (`data/config/protocols.json`, 32 protocols), checked against both hulls:

| Protocols | Modifier | Zone ids | Valid on mega | Valid on Explorer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOP-007, SOP-009 | `close_zones` | `Main_Galley_Aft`, `Crew_Mess_Main` | both | neither |
| SOP-017 variants (10) | `exempt_work_zones` | 11 or 19 ids | all | `Bridge` (and `Windjammer` in the WIDE variant) only |

No other protocol names a zone id. The SOP-017 variants trigger on `scenario_calendar`, which no cell of this campaign declares.

What a valid Explorer mapping would be, for any response that becomes active: SOP-007 and SOP-009 close the main galley and the crew mess, which on Explorer are `MainGalley`, and `CrewMess` with `CrewMessOff`. These ids exist on the committed platform. The mapping is recorded, not applied.

Can the frozen baseline switch response actions off consistently on both hulls?

- **Not through the frozen block.** No key of the block disables a protocol; SOP-007 opens at `SUSPECTED` and SOP-009 at `LOCKDOWN`, and the block pins those thresholds.
- **Yes through the run spec.** `catalog.protocols_path` selects the protocol bundle per run, on any platform (`picard_framework/run_spec.py`; the COVID platform-fidelity campaign ships its own bundle this way). A bundle without the two closure SOPs, or with per-platform closure ids, would apply identically to Explorer and to the archetypes. It is a new data file, not an edit of `protocols.json`.
- **Not exercised here.** Using a different bundle on mega moves it off the reference the joint evaluation runs, so it is a freeze-level decision for the reviewer, not a campaign choice.

What this campaign does instead: `cell.py` records the first epoch every protocol is active, and the readout counts the cells where SOP-007 or SOP-009 activated. In such a cell the two hulls respond differently from that epoch on (SOP-009's confinement scalars apply on both; its closures only on mega), and the Explorer-against-mega difference in that cell is confounded by it.

## Fruit-cluster gate

**Verdict: the per-day onset curves are not published. No second hull is scoped.**

Rispens et al., "Notes from the Field: Multiple Cruise Ship Outbreaks of Norovirus Associated with Frozen Fruits and Berries — United States, 2019", MMWR 2020;69(16), DOI 10.15585/mmwr.mm6916a3 (PMC7188409).

- Where read: the whole body text (Europe PMC full-text XML), cross-checked against Consensus full-text excerpts. The article has no figure, no table and no supplementary material.
- What it reports: voyage-end totals for the three U.S.-jurisdiction voyages (117/2,046 and 8/610; 85/2,166 and 10/612; 83/2,251 and 10/610), notification and investigation dates, laboratory and traceback results. No illness onsets by day or by role.
- Not checked: whether an unpublished VSP field report for those voyages contains an epidemic curve. That is an agency document outside the gate as written.
