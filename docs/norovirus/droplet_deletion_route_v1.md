# What the droplet deletion does: the matched arm measurement

**Status: measurement of record for the deleted droplet route.** It supersedes
[route_attribution_headcount_scaling.md](route_attribution_headcount_scaling.md)
as a description of current behaviour; that document remains the record of the
pre-deletion engine. Ledger items 39, 40 and 41.

`hull_compounding_route_v1` re-run twice on **one** image
(`droplet-emission-deletion-76c0c51`, job definition `picard-campaign:40`), the
two arms differing only in `transmission.droplet_emission_mode`:

- **`deleted`** — `profile_conditioned`, the engine default. An
  `emesis_conditioned` profile contributes **no** continuous emission to the
  room droplet pool, cabin-mate addback or near field.
- **`shipped`** — `shipped_uniform`, the pre-deletion uniform
  `DROPLET_AEROSOL_FRACTION = 0.05` on every arm. It exists so the deletion is
  measured against a matched baseline rather than against #533's archive, which
  was generated by a different build.

Both arms: classic and spirit architecture at 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 of declared
complement, 7-day voyages, the default realism boarding chain, the same 300
seeds (7500–7799) per cell, identical run ids by construction and therefore
separate output roots and S3 prefixes. 1,800 runs each; AWS Batch
`7ff847c0-72a0-4633-af29-c9b210a3ed57` (deleted) and
`a928d39c-5a3b-45da-8729-9d4994fadddb` (shipped), 128/128 shards each, 0 failed,
3,600/3,600 runs. **Nothing was fitted, no constant was re-valued and no
replacement droplet fraction was proposed.**

Away from declared complement these cells remain a **mechanism probe**: the
0.25 and 0.5 cells carry no posting, attack-rate or anchor claim.

## 1. The level collapses; the exponent does not

Secondaries per import, matched seeds:

| platform | agents | imports | shipped | deleted | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| classic | 478 | 1.08 | 0.375 | 0.012 | 32× |
| classic | 955 | 2.15 | 2.420 | 0.195 | 12× |
| classic | 1,910 | 4.37 | 6.601 | 0.134 | **49×** |
| spirit | 750 | 1.75 | 0.752 | 0.025 | 30× |
| spirit | 1,500 | 3.49 | 1.929 | 0.050 | 39× |
| spirit | 3,000 | 7.04 | 7.954 | 0.257 | **31×** |

Total secondaries over each cell's 300 voyages fall from 8,647 to 176 (classic,
full) and from 16,790 to 543 (spirit, full). Zero-secondary voyages rise from
21–35% to 91–99%.

The headcount exponent, quarter → full complement, **survives the deletion**:

| platform | shipped | deleted |
|---|---:|---:|
| classic | N^2.07 | N^1.72 |
| spirit | N^1.70 | N^1.69 |

This is the structural point. The exponent was never a property of the droplet
constant; it is a property of any route whose dose per person is
N-independent while the number of people receiving it scales with N. Fomite is
such a route, and it inherits the scaling unchanged — off a base 30–50× lower.
Deleting the droplet share removed the **magnitude** of the large-hull problem,
not its mechanism.

Two cautions on the deleted arm's own numbers. Classic is **non-monotonic**
(0.012 → 0.195 → 0.134), so its exponent is read across the 4× endpoint step
rather than fitted. And the remaining transmission is extremely concentrated:
the top 1% of voyages carry **63–100%** of every deleted cell's secondaries
(against 8–44% shipped), so these means are dominated by a handful of voyages
— maxima of 48 (classic, full) and 138 (spirit, full) against a 90th percentile
of 0 in every deleted cell. The level is measured; the exponent is indicative.

## 3. Posting and attack rate against the observed series

| platform | agents | shipped post/1,000 | deleted post/1,000 | deleted 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| classic | 478 | 0.0 | 0.0 | [0, 8.3] |
| classic | 955 | 20.0 | 0.0 | [0, 8.3] |
| classic | 1,910 | 36.7 | **0.0** | [0, 8.3] |
| spirit | 750 | 0.0 | 0.0 | [0, 8.3] |
| spirit | 1,500 | 6.7 | 0.0 | [0, 8.3] |
| spirit | 3,000 | 46.7 | **6.7** | [1.4, 21.2] |

Posting is zero in five of six cells. The surviving cell posts twice in 300
voyages, one of them on the crew channel only.

**The prediction recorded in ledger item 40 — "posting collapses well below
A9" — is not what happened, and is corrected here.** A9 is 4.19–5.58 postings
per 1,000 voyages fleet-pooled and 0.33 per 1,000 in the 6–7 day band the
campaign's 7-day cells occupy. The deleted arm's 0/300 has a 95% Jeffreys
interval of [0, 8.3] per 1,000, which **contains both**. At 300 voyages per
cell the deleted arm is not resolvable *either* side of A9: the honest statement
is that the unsourced 0.05 was carrying a 110–140× overshoot at full
complement, and removing it leaves posting frequency no longer distinguishable
from the observed series rather than beneath it.

What *is* now too low is outbreak **size** given a posting. Median reported
passenger attack rate over the deleted arm's two spirit postings is 0.0153,
against A4's classic/spirit medians of 0.053–0.055 (IQR 0.042–0.078). The
unconditional distribution loses its upper tail, not merely its threshold
grazes: the worst voyage in 300 falls from a reported passenger attack rate of
0.0561 to 0.0067 (classic, full) and from 0.0567 to 0.0305 (spirit, full), and
the 90th percentile of secondary infections is **0** in every deleted cell.

## 4. What this does and does not establish

It establishes that one unsourced constant carried 85–99% of all established
infection and the entire magnitude of the large-hull problem, and that the model
without it is an under-producer of outbreaks rather than an over-producer.

It establishes nothing about validity. Deletion removed a defect that was doing
the work of mechanisms the model does not have; the resulting agreement on
posting frequency is an interval too wide to be agreement, and the disagreement
on outbreak size is now the binding finding. The two named gaps are unchanged
and remain separate changes: toilet-flush aerosolisation (the
diarrhoea-conditioned air source this model has no lavatory zone or toilet-use
event for) and the droplet route's continued disregard of a **continuous**
arm's declared `airborne_emission_fraction` — a 15× move on COVID, measured on
its own arm or not at all. Zero food-contamination establishments across 3,600
cruise voyages also remains an open reading of VSP investigation practice.
